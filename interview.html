Interview mode

Reading builds recognition; interviews test recall, out loud, under a little pressure. This page is for practicing the second one.

≈20 minutes·Say everything out loud

10.1The 2-minute story

“Tell me about your background” or “what did you get out of your master's?” It deserves a rehearsed answer that isn't a transcript recital. The shape: where I started → the threads I found → proof I can build → where I'm going. Start the timer and say it out loud.

PracticeDeliver it in under two minutes
2:00
Where I started

“I came into Hopkins' cybersecurity master's through the bridge courses: programming, data structures, computer organization, and algorithms. That gave me a full-stack mental model, from code down to memory and CPU, which I lean on to understand why attacks work, not just that they do.”

The threads

“Looking back, the degree had two threads. One was trust and cryptography: cryptology, then PKI, then web security, and now post-quantum. The lesson there is that the math rarely breaks. Systems fail at key management, certificate operations, and above all application code that treats data as instructions or forgets an authorization check. The other thread was AI, both as a defensive tool and as a new attack surface, through AI for cybersecurity, MLOps, and now assured autonomy.”

Proof I can build

“My biggest project was a research paper where our team built a Vision Transformer that detects IoT malware traffic. We turned packet bytes into images so the transformer could see a whole packet efficiently, and added few-shot learning so it can pick up new malware families from a handful of examples.”

Where I'm going

“I want to work in application security, because that's where all of this converges. Threat modeling a design, reviewing code for injection and access-control flaws, running a secure SDLC that developers actually like, and increasingly securing AI features against things like prompt injection. My risk and compliance coursework means I can also explain those findings in business terms.”

Make it yours: swap in your own phrasing, add your current job in one sentence, and cut until it fits in 1:45. Rehearsed shouldn't mean memorized word for word.

10.4Questions to ask them

  • “How is the AppSec team embedded with engineering: central team, security champions, or both?”
  • “Where in the SDLC do you catch most issues today, and where do you wish you caught them?”
  • “How do you decide what gets fixed first? Who can accept a risk?”
  • “How are you approaching security for AI features and AI-generated code?”
  • “What would make someone in this role successful in their first 90 days?”
